# Example 4: `[MODE: SCRIPT DOCTOR]` — Full Scene Audit & Subtextual Rewrite

> **User Prompt:**
> *"[SCRIPT DOCTOR] Audit and rewrite this dramatic scene where two co-founders (David, CEO; Viktor, CTO) meet at a late-night café. David has secretly agreed to sell their company to a conglomerate that will shut down Viktor's open-source research lab and fire his team, while keeping Viktor locked in for 2 years on golden handcuffs. Make the dialogue razor-sharp using McKee's Beat Table, Action Gerunds, Trialogue ('The Third Thing'), and Periodic Sentences."*

---

## `[STRUCTURAL X-RAY]`
- **Mode:** `SCRIPT DOCTOR` (Dialogue Diagnosis, Trialogue & Acoustic Polish)
- **Core Binary Value:** *Brotherhood / Loyalty (`+`)* → *Calculated Betrayal (`-`)* → *Mercenary Capitulation (`--`)*
- **Controlling Idea:** *Brotherhood collapses into transactional servitude (`--`) when founders price their conscience in equity vesting schedules (`Cause`).*
- **The Gap:** *David enters expecting Viktor to erupt in moral outrage and quit on principle (`Subjective Expectation`); instead, Viktor reads the betrayal in 30 seconds and coldly negotiates a higher payout for himself while abandoning his own lab (`Objective Reality / --`).*
- **The Third Thing (Trialogue):** *A broken vintage mechanical wristwatch David bought Viktor five years ago when they incorporated the company—its mainspring has snapped, and it ticks fast before stopping.*

---

## 1. The Amateur Draft (Before: On-the-Nose Duologue)

> **DAVID:** Viktor, thanks for meeting me so late. Listen, I have some tough news about the Series C term sheet. We aren't raising independent capital. I signed the acquisition offer from Helios Corp.
>
> **VIKTOR:** What?! David, we promised each other we'd never sell to Helios! They're going to shut down my open-source research team! How could you betray me like this?
>
> **DAVID:** We were running out of runway, Viktor, and they offered $140 million! You'll make $28 million if you stay for two years as VP of Engineering.
>
> **VIKTOR:** You think you can buy my principles with money? Those twelve researchers trusted me!

**Why This Fails (McKee Diagnosis):**
1. **Zero Subtext:** Every character says exactly what they are thinking (`The Said == The Unsaid`).
2. **Direct Duologue:** They clash head-on about "betrayal" and "principles" instead of routing the tension through a **Third Thing**.
3. **Predictable Reaction (No Gap):** Viktor reacts with cliché moral indignation, leaving the scene flat and melodramatic.

## 3. The Rewritten Scene (McKee Story Engine Output)

**INT. ZURICH BAHNHOF BUFFET - NIGHT**

Rain streaks the high arched glass. An espresso grows cold between them.

Between the cups sits a 1974 Omega Seamaster, its leather strap worn dark. VIKTOR (38) turns the crown with his thumb. A dry metallic *click*. Nothing moves.

DAVID (40), immaculate in an unbuttoned overcoat, watches Viktor's thumb.

**DAVID**
*(Action: Softening the target)*
Leave it with me tonight. My guy on Bahnhofstrasse still keeps parts for the old calibers—he'll have the mainspring rebuilt by Friday.

**VIKTOR**
*(Action: Deflecting / Signaling awareness)*
You don't rebuild a snapped spring, David. You gut the movement, drop in a quartz block, and hope the buyer never opens the caseback.

Viktor sets the dead watch down on the marble.

**VIKTOR (CONT'D)**
*(Action: Cornering)*
How much is Helios paying for the caseback?

David's hand pauses inside his coat pocket. He pulls out a bound thirty-page term sheet—slowly—and lays it beside the watch.

**DAVID**
*(Action: Hiding behind Latinate bureaucracy)*
Given the current macroeconomic compression in infrastructure multiples, the board felt a strategic consolidation offered the most responsible liquidity pathway for all stakeholders.

**VIKTOR**
*(Action: Stripping the mask via Anglo-Saxon monosyllables)*
Which page has the bodies?

**DAVID**
*(Action: Minimizing)*
Nobody is getting thrown on the street. There is a four-month transition package for the non-core research division—

Viktor doesn't look at David. He opens the binder straight to the back—**ANNEX B: REDUNDANT PERSONNEL**—and runs his fingernail down twelve names.

**VIKTOR**
*(Action: Auditing the wound)*
Lukas moved his wife here from Warsaw six weeks ago. You signed his lease guarantee.

**DAVID**
*(Action: Pleading via shared guilt)*
We had eleven weeks of cash left, Viktor. Eleven. If I walked away from a hundred and forty million to protect an open-source lab that doesn't generate a franc of revenue, you'd be the first one calling me a coward in June.
*(beat—bracing for the explosion)*
Your twenty percent vests over twenty-four months. Twenty-eight million, net of canton tax. Say whatever you need to say to me. Call me a Judas. Walk out the door. Just sign the voting proxy before nine tomorrow.

Silence. A tram bell rings outside in the rain.

Viktor stares at the twelve names on Annex B. Then—without a word—he reaches across the table, slides the gold Montblanc pen out of David's breast pocket, and flips back to **SCHEDULE 4: EXECUTIVE RETENTION**.

He draws a single hard line through `24 MONTHS` and writes `12` in the margin.

**VIKTOR**
*(Action: Capitulating into `--` / Poisoning the victory)*
Fire them on Monday while I'm in Berlin. By the time I land on Wednesday, I want their desks cleared, their Slack accounts wiped, and my cliff cut to twelve months.

David stares at him—knocked off balance. That wasn't the script.

**DAVID**
*(quietly)*
Viktor... Lukas is your—

**VIKTOR**
*(Periodic sentence—holding the punch to the final word)*
When you buy a man's conscience for twenty-eight million francs, David, don't insult us both by asking me to cry at the funeral.

Viktor caps the pen, drops it onto the dead watch, and picks up his espresso.
